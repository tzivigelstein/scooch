# English (`src/copy/en.ts`)

US English, written as a native UI writer would: the same voice as the
Spanish guide ([../copy.md](../copy.md)), adapted to English conventions.
**Needs a review by a native speaker.**

## Conventions

- Sentence case everywhere: «Download PDF», «Keyboard shortcuts».
- Second person, present tense, plain verbs. Contractions are fine
  («Can’t align», «Doesn’t fit»).
- Curly quotes and apostrophes: “Cards”, don’t. No guillemets.
- No space before %: «100%». A no-break space between a number and its
  unit: «10 mm», «300 ppi». Decimal point: «2.5 cm». Thousands in running
  text with a comma («1,000%»); never in measurements, which stay readable
  by the fields («1189 mm»).
- Results of an action in the past tense with the verb first: «Arranged 7
  items», «Removed card.jpg». States with a participle: «Rotated 90°».
- Photo sizes in inches («4 × 6 in»), as English-speaking photo labs name
  them; ID photos keep their centimeters.
- Key names as printed on the keys: Ctrl, Shift, Alt, Enter, Space, Delete,
  Backspace, Esc, Page Up, Page Down; ⌘ ⌥ ⇧ ⌃ on Apple devices, said
  Command, Option, Shift, Control.

## Site pages

The guides in `src/site/content/en` follow the same conventions, with the
app’s names from `en.ts` (but the legal pages are “Terms of use” and
“Privacy policy” there: the app’s labels say “(in Spanish)” because it links
to the Spanish pages).

- The print dialog’s options as browsers name them: “Actual size” or
  “100%”, “Fit to page”, “Fit to printable area”.
- A photo size before “photos” goes without its unit, as people search for
  it: “4 × 6 photos”; elsewhere, the app’s name: “cropped to 4 × 6 in”.
  When a count would touch it, the size goes after: “3 photos of 4 × 6 in”.
- Margins and spacing in millimeters, as the editor shows them; resolution
  in ppi.
